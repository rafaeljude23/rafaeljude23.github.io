Fixing Broken B2B Travel Payments: The Power of Virtual Cards

Learn More

Why B2B Travel Payments Are Broken

Traditional travel B2B payments were characterized by manual processes, heavy reliance on paper checks and traditional wire transfers, and slow settlement periods (10–14 days).

This pass-through framework created significant overhead costs, friction-prone reconciliation, and exposure to currency fluctuations that squeezed agency profit margins., requiring online travel agencies, TMCs, and tour operators to constantly settle payments across sprawling airline, hotel, and rail networks.

Yet, this fast-moving industry runs on fragmented financial infrastructure, exposing businesses to a costly triple threat: manual reconciliation, high fees, and severe fraud vulnerabilities.

Without unique tracking codes, finance teams face administrative gridlock. Staff must spend countless hours cross-referencing generic bank statements with internal booking IDs just to close the month. Simultaneously, cross-border payment complexities—including volatile rates, hidden fees, and wire charges—silently inflate international transaction costs by 5% to 7%, heavily eroding thin profit margins.

Outdated billing methods create severe security gaps and operational friction. Relying on static corporate card numbers across hundreds of global suppliers invites card-not-present fraud, hidden overcharges, and rogue spending. This legacy approach forces back offices to waste hours on manual reconciliation and drives travelers to pay out of pocket—adding needless risk to an environment that must move at modern speed.

What is Travel B2B Virtual Card

Virtual credit cards (VCNs) are purely electronic payment credentials generated for specific transactions. Unlike physical plastic cards, each digital VCN contains its own unique card number, CVV, and expiration date.

In the travel sector, these cards are central to the Merchant of Record (MoR) model, where a travel agency collects payment from a customer and then uses unique VCNs to pay individual suppliers, such as airlines and hotels. This technology is rapidly becoming the industry standard, with projections suggesting the B2B segment will represent 85% of the global virtual card market by 2030.

How It Works

The lifecycle of a virtual card in a corporate travel environment generally follows these steps:

  1. A traveller confirms an itinerary through an approved travel management platform.
  2. The system automatically generates a unique VCN tied to that specific booking.
  3. The business pre-configures the card with strict controls, such as exact spend limits, specific merchant categories, and a narrow validity window.
  4. The OTA or travel manager uses the VCN to pay the supplier.
  5. When the supplier charges the card, the platform assesses the request against the pre-set controls in real time.
  6. Because the VCN is mapped one-to-one with a specific booking, the transaction is automatically recorded and matched in the company’s accounting or ERP system.

Key Features of Virtual Cards

Virtual cards offer advanced technical features that traditional cards cannot match:

  • Single-Use Credentials: Fresh credentials are created for every transaction, rendering stolen data useless for future use.
  • Spending Controls: Organizations can set merchant category code (MCC) restrictions, geographic limits, and specific expiration dates.
  • Multi-Currency Capabilities: Businesses can hold balances and pay in the supplier’s local currency, bypassing expensive FX markups.
  • Rich Data Capture: VCNs provide Level 3 data, capturing over 130 data elements per transaction, including GL codes, cost centers, and business purposes.
  • Authorization Relay: Some platforms allow businesses to maintain full control by approving or declining payments at the exact moment of authorization.

Benefits to Travel Businesses

Adopting virtual cards delivers measurable improvements across security and operations:

  • Unparalleled Fraud Prevention: VCNs carry a 30x lower risk of fraud compared to traditional payment cards due to their single-use nature and tokenization.
  • Automated Reconciliation: The one-to-one link between a card and a booking eliminates manual spreadsheet cross-referencing, supporting a faster month-end close.
  • Significant Cost Savings: By paying in local currencies and avoiding FX conversion fees, businesses can typically save 3% on international transactions.
  • Real-Time Spend Visibility: Finance teams gain a current view of travel spend as it happens, allowing for proactive budget adjustments rather than reactive auditing.
  • New Revenue Streams: Businesses can often earn a share of interchange revenue on their B2B card transactions, turning a cost center into a profit generator.
